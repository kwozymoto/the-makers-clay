# Updating the website

You don't need to touch any code. Everything is edited through a website called
Pages CMS, which saves your changes and updates the site for you.

**You need:** a GitHub account (the one the site lives under) and a browser.
It works on a phone, which is handy for adding photos.

---

## Signing in, the first time

1. Go to **[app.pagescms.org](https://app.pagescms.org)**
2. Click **Sign in with GitHub** and allow it access
3. Pick **the-makers-clay** from your list of projects

Bookmark that page. Next time it's just sign in and go.

---

## Adding a new piece

1. Click **Pieces**, then **Add an entry**
2. **Photos** — upload them. The first one is the one people see in the shop,
   so put the best one first. Upright photos look better than wide ones.
   Upload the full-size photo; the website shrinks it for you.
3. **Name (English)** — short. This also becomes the web address.
4. **Price** — just the number. Leave it empty to show "Price on enquiry".
5. **Is it available?** and **Is there only one?** — pick whichever fits.
6. Click **Save**. That's enough for a piece to go live.

Description, size and care sit below that, and every Chinese field is grouped
together at the bottom — so you can do the whole English pass first and decide
about the Chinese one separately. Anything left blank falls back to English.

The piece appears on the site about a minute later. See
[When will I see my changes?](#when-will-i-see-my-changes) below.

## The things you'll do most often

### Something sold

Open the piece → **Is it available?** → **Sold** → Save.

Leave it on the site. A sold piece still does work: it shows people what you
make, and the page invites them to ask about something similar. A shop with a
few sold pieces looks better than an empty one.

### Something is out of stock but you can make another

Open the piece → **Is it available?** → **Out of stock — can make another** →
Save. It stays listed and says "Made to order".

### Change a price

Open the piece → change **Price** → Save.

### Put something on the front page

Open the piece → turn on **Show on the home page** → Save. Three or four
pieces there is about right.

### Change the order things appear in

**Position in the shop** — higher numbers come first. Most pieces can stay at
0; just give the one you want at the top a higher number, like 100.

## Editing the About page

Click **About page**. There's an English section and a Chinese one, each with a
heading and a list of paragraphs. Add or remove paragraphs with the buttons
beside them.

This is the page people read before deciding to buy from someone they've never
met, so it's worth having it sound like you.

---

## Site settings

Click **Site settings**.

- **Hide the site from Google** — leave this **on** while you're still setting
  up. It shows a "work in progress" bar and keeps the site out of search
  results. Turn it **off** when you're ready for people to find you.
- **WhatsApp number** — country code first, digits only, no + or spaces. So
  012-345 6789 becomes **60123456789**. Until this is filled in, the enquiry
  buttons send people to Instagram instead.
- **Instagram**, **Email** — shown in the footer.

## When will I see my changes?

**About a minute.** When you hit Save, the website rebuilds itself in the
background. There's no progress bar, so:

- Save once. Don't keep clicking it.
- Wait a minute, then refresh the site.
- If it still looks old, refresh again — your browser may be showing you a
  saved copy.
- Still nothing after a few minutes? Click **Rebuild the site** in the sidebar
  and give it another minute.

---

## Things worth knowing

**Nothing you do is permanent.** Every change is saved with a history. If you
delete something by accident or change your mind, it can be brought back
exactly as it was. Don't be afraid of the Save button.

**Photos are the thing that sells pottery.** The website can make a decent
photo look good, but it can't rescue a dark one. Natural light near a window,
plain background, and the piece filling most of the frame.

**Two languages, one piece.** You don't create a piece twice. Each piece has
English fields and Chinese fields side by side in the same form. Fill in as
much as you want — anything left blank falls back to English.

---

## If something looks broken

Nothing here can be permanently broken, so don't panic. Take a screenshot of
what you're seeing and send it on, along with what you'd just done. The site's
history makes it straightforward to work out what happened and undo it.
